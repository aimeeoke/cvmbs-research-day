# Reviewing Abstracts as a Faculty Mentor
**CVMBS Research Day · January 23, 2027 · researchday.vercel.app**

If a student, trainee, or lab member has listed you as their faculty mentor on a Research Day abstract, you can review, edit, and approve it directly in the site — no separate access request, no permission to grant. This guide covers the setup and the few edge cases that trip people up.

---

## The short version

1. **Sign up** at **researchday.vercel.app** using your `first.last@colostate.edu` CSU alias. Choose "**CVMBS Faculty**" when prompted.
2. **Sign in** — a 6-digit code is emailed to you each time. There is no password to create or remember.
3. **Click "Abstracts"** in the sidebar. Every abstract that names you as a mentor appears here — not just ones you created yourself.
4. **Click Edit** on any row. You see the full submit form, all fields editable, exactly what the submitter sees.

That's the whole flow. If something isn't working, the rest of this guide covers why.

---

## How the site decides what you can see

When a trainee drafts their abstract, they pick you from a dropdown of CVMBS faculty. That dropdown is pre-loaded with every CVMBS faculty member's `first.last@colostate.edu` address.

When **you** sign up and verify your account with that same email, the site automatically links you to every abstract that lists you — current and future.

> **That's why the exact email matters.** The CVMBS faculty picker uses your `first.last@colostate.edu` alias. If you sign up for Research Day with a different address — `jsmith@colostate.edu`, a Gmail, a departmental alias — the site has no way to connect you to the abstracts that list you. If you're unsure which address is in the faculty roster, assume it's `first.last@colostate.edu` and sign up with that, even if it's not the inbox you normally check. CSU forwards it to your real inbox, and the 6-digit login codes will arrive there.

## The Abstract Portal

Once signed in, the **Abstracts** page (sidebar) lists everything you can access. Each row shows:

| Column | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Presenter** | Who is actually giving the talk or standing by the poster |
| **Title** | The abstract title (italics and subscripts preserved) |
| **Status** | `Draft` → `Submitted` → `Finalized` (or `Withdrawn`) |
| **My role** | How the site classifies you on this abstract: `Mentor`, `Submitter`, `Presenter`, or `Author` |
| **Action** | Edit, View, or more via the three-dot menu |

Where you're a mentor, the edit form shows a blue banner at the top: *"You're editing as mentor/presenter."* Your changes save the same as the submitter's.

## What you can and can't do

- **Before Nov 16 (submission deadline):** anything the submitter can do — edit text, reorder authors, swap mentors, change session preference. Only the submitter sees the green **Submit** button, though. If a draft is ready and the submitter has gone silent, email me at aimee.oke@colostate.edu and I'll push it through.
- **Between Nov 16 and Jan 4:** the abstract is locked in for scheduling (the presenter's slot is reserved), but you can still edit the title, abstract text, author list, funding, and so on.
- **After Jan 4 (finalize deadline):** everything locks. Edits require my intervention.

## Supporting your trainees

Common things trainees run into:

- **"I can't find my abstract when I log in."** They need to sign in with the same email the submitter typed into the Presenter row. If someone else created the draft and typed a different email, the site treats them as a different person. Fix: edit the Presenter email on the draft.
- **"The Mentor picker doesn't have you."** If your name is missing from the CVMBS faculty picker, reply to the committee's reminder email — we'll add you to the roster. (The picker is pre-loaded from HR's faculty list and may be out of date for recent hires or affiliate appointments.)
- **"I picked you but you say you can't see it."** Most often: the trainee picked you from the dropdown, but you signed up with a different email alias. Check that both ends match.
